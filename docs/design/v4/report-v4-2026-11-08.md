## 1. The three cuts

v4 is one direction taken three times: move work out of scripts that sequence the run and into the host's own mechanisms, keep only what a
prompt cannot be trusted to do.

| Cut | What it did | Outcome | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Single loop** (2026-11-07) | the run stopped being a launched workflow script; `building` became a loop in the PO's own session, planning steps folded into a fence-only `plan` activity, five skills and a courier deleted | every activity done with a real run behind it; the release (`v4.0.0`) was left local by the PO's decision | `execution-2026-11-07.md` |
| **2. Native first** (2026-11-08, S1–S2) | workers became plugin subagents (`agents/*.md`, `skills:` preload), the dispatch receipt came from the host, the Stop hook and several kernel verbs went | S1 and S2 each gated on a real run that shipped; one defect in S2 was found by S3 and fixed | `execution-native-first-2026-11-08.md` |
| **3. Engine removed** (S3 measured, then removed) | `compile`, `reduce`, `probe`, three `verify` verbs and the helper libraries deleted; the loop sequences itself | the removal was applied after S3 measured the engine ahead; one real run on the result shipped, after a resume | same record |

A fourth, small change follows from the real run on the third: GATE L4 refuses `ship` over a QA hunt the run owed and never ran.

What this licenses: the engine shipped more cheaply and more reliably, in every run it was given. What it does not license: a rate (n is

refusal) have not themselves been run.

**Found on 2026-11-09, reading the archived receipts: the comparison is confounded by model.** Every launch record asked for `sonnet`,
and the execution record calls the S3 arms "same model, same day". The dispatch receipts, which carry the model the host reported for
each worker, say otherwise: both engine arms ran their workers on `claude-sonnet-5-5`, and every prose-loop arm (B1, the earlier variant-B
launch, and the two engine-removed sessions) ran them on `claude-opus-5-5`. The agents use `model: inherit`, so the worker's model is the
launching session's, and the launch record is a request the run does not enforce. The "59 % higher cost" and the $18.2 therefore mix
two prices with two loops, and the receipts cannot say how much of the gap is the model. The direction of the reliability result (2 of 2
against 0 of 1, then 1 of 1 after a resume) is not explained away by a stronger model on the losing side, but the cost figures should not
be read as a measurement of the engine's worth until one arm is re-run on the other arm's model. This is also the concrete case for the
row 3 note below: the receipts, not the launch record, name what ran.

## 4. What the removal kept, and what it handed to the loop

Kept and executed by tests: the receipt (`init run`), the gate resolver (`gate`, including the L2 empty-board refusal and the L4 census and
QA refusals), the T0 witness (`verify t0`), the write fence (`sandbox-guard`, armed and retired by `order open | close`) and the dispatch
receipts, plus `verify envelope`, `safety-spine`, the four oracles and the installer.

Moved from a script to the loop's own discipline, checked only by the PO and the evaluator: choosing the next step, applying a worker's
result to the board, linting the spec, counting attempts against the budget, building and launching before EVAL, the census and the ship
report. `AGENTS.md` says so in its first section.
