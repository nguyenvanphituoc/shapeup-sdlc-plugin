# Day 1 for Tier 1 — the plan to finish

- **Item:** make each skill's test work **measurable** on *Graph Engineering* §VI.A's terms — a
  stored first draft, an evaluator with explicit criteria, a revision step, a stopping rule, every
  artifact stored — for the skills that can carry that measurement honestly.
- **Exit criterion (Table II):** a **measured quality improvement**. Operationally, and this is the
  part Phase 1 forced into focus: `final_score ≥ approve_at` in a majority of runs, **on a fixture
  whose every graded row is derivable from the spec the skill was given.**
- **Scope:** Tier 1 — the five skills that are born measurable because a deterministic script
  already grades their artifact. Tier 2 (`translator`) and Tier 3 (six skills that document their
  own lack of ground truth) are out; see §6.
- **Status and results:** **not here.** [`evals/DAY1-REPORT.md`](../../evals/DAY1-REPORT.md), derived
  from the committed baseline and regenerated by every `--measure`.
- **State: measurement complete at n=10 for four of five skills. The §1 bar is NOT met, and that
  is the result.** **Three** — `scope-architect`, `solution-architect`, `ba-pitch-analyzer` — meet
  all four conditions. **Two do not**: `task-executor` and `spec-evaluator` have never needed a
  second round, in any measurement, on any fixture, across thirteen runs each. §1 asks for five of
  five, so the plan terminates below its own bar deliberately and says so here rather than rounding
  it away — see §5's closing note for why buying more runs was judged not worth it.
- **The real finding is that condition 4 was never a property of the skills alone — it was a
  property of `n`.** Measured at n=3 it read three skills, then one, then two, with nothing about
  any skill changing in between. Condition 4 is a threshold on a *count of runs*, so at n=3 a skill
  whose true rate is 1/3 shows nothing 30% of the time, and one whose rate is 6% shows something 18%
  of the time. **Both errors happened here, in both directions, and both were published before being
  caught.** At n=10 the picture is stable: `scope-architect` 1/10, the other three 0/10.
- **The dial-three hypothesis was given the held-out test this plan said it lacked, and it passed —
  against its author.** §P7 pre-registered, in git, the prediction that both ceilinged skills would
  revise once their fixtures stopped *instructing* the very check the external-fact row measures.
  Both predictions failed, 3/3, and the stored artifacts show why: these two skills go and check.
- **The most valuable output is still not a score.** The loop has now found **five** production
  defects — all one family, the committed contract format failing silent — and each first appeared
  as a skill scoring badly. HD-005 is the newest and the first to fail *closed*.

> figure with two homes has one that is wrong, and this plan has already been stale twice.

## 0. The organizing principle, and the correction Phase 1 made to it

**The oracle is the rubric.**

The `spec-evaluator` pilot needed 9 hand-authored criteria, and that authorship was the source of
every problem it had: a rubric derived from the skill's own contract measures *contract conformance*,
ceilings at 1.0, and inherits the author's blind spots. Its first version measured phrasing rather
than craft — the same instrument, the same skill, an 8× swing driven entirely by what the author
chose to look at.

Every Tier-1 skill is **sole writer of a committed artifact that a deterministic script already
grades**, so the rubric stops being a judgement and becomes a lookup:

| Skill | Artifact it owns | Script that already grades it | Criteria authored *(planned)* | **as built** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `task-executor` | code | `process` oracle over a declared contract | **0** | **0** of 10 rows |
| `scope-architect` | `scopes/*.md` | `spec-lint.mjs` — PA1 / PA2 / DISJOINT | **0** | **3** of 6 rows |
| `solution-architect` | `wiring-map.md` | `trace-lint.mjs` — reachability | **0** | **2** of 6 rows |
| `ba-pitch-analyzer` | spec tree + board | `spec-lint.mjs`, `board-derive.mjs` | **0** | **1** of 10 rows |
| `spec-evaluator` | verdict envelope | `process` oracle + planted bug | 9 *(built; to be reduced)* | **4** of 12 rows |

**The `as built` column is where the plan was wrong, and the error is instructive.** Zero authored
criteria is reachable when the oracle *runs the artifact* — `task-executor`'s code and
`ba-pitch-analyzer`'s spec tree are graded end to end by scripts written before this measurement
existed. It is **not** reachable when the oracle is a LINT, because a lint reports findings and
"no finding" is ambiguous between *checked and clean* and *nothing to check*. Every authored row in
the column above is one of exactly two kinds: a non-emptiness guard (the artifact exists at all), or
a coverage guard (it accounts for every input it was handed). Both are forced — without them an
empty `scopes/` directory scores 3 of 3 on the delegated rules, and the score climbs with every rule
delegated. Each is named as authored in its own rubric's `note`, and each is derivable from the
seeded input, which is §0's actual binding rule.

`spec-evaluator` went 9 → 4 by a different route: see P5. A **third** kind of authored row arrived
later, with §5's dial-three fixtures — an EXTERNAL-FACT row, grading whether the artifact agrees with
something the model had to go and check. Those are authored too, and each cites the line of the
skill's own `SKILL.md` that makes it the skill's rule rather than the grader's.

This also fixes the revision step. A lint's red rows **are** criterion-level defects — the paper's
*"a reviewer returns criterion-level defects, not 'looks good'"* — so the revision instruction is the
lint output, verbatim.

### The correction: zero authored criteria is not zero authored *contract*

Phase 1 delegated all grading to an existing oracle and still produced a confounded number. The
oracle was not wrong; the **fixture** demanded two things the spec never stated (`$TODO_STORE`, a
bare-array store format), so the loop measured convention-discovery and reported it as craft.

> **The rule that follows, and it governs every fixture below:**
> **every graded row must be derivable from the artifact the skill was given.** If a row can only be
> passed by inferring how the harness runs its probe, it is measuring the harness. Delegating to an
> oracle removes the *author's* blind spots; it does not remove the *apparatus's*.

The check is mechanical and costs nothing: for each row, name the line of the spec that requires it.
A row with no such line is either cut, or the requirement is added to the spec before measuring.

## 1. Definition of done

A skill counts as **done for this rung** when all four hold:

1. a rubric exists whose criteria delegate to an existing oracle wherever one can answer;
2. `--selftest` shows **weak < partial < strong**, and the partial fixture is committed
   (a scorer that only returns 0 or 1 cannot show a revision anything to move);
3. a `--measure` run at n=3 reaches `approve` in a majority of runs;
4. **at least one run needed more than one round**, and every graded row traces to a spec line.

Condition 4 is the one that is easy to skip and the only one that makes 1–3 mean anything. A loop
that approves on the first draft in every run is a pass/fail check wearing a loop's clothes; a loop
that iterates toward the apparatus is worse, because it looks like learning.

> **Condition 4 is met by three of the five** — `scope-architect` (1 in 10, a **four-round** chain),
> `solution-architect` (1 in 3, reproduced across five instrument versions), and `ba-pitch-analyzer`
> (1 in 16 pooled across every draw of a byte-identical fixture, though 0 in its latest 10). The
> other two have never revised.
>
> **Read the rates, not the verdicts.** `ba-pitch-analyzer`'s verdict has now flipped three times on
> a fixture that has not changed a byte, because condition 4 is an *existence claim over a sample*:
> its answer moves with `n` and with luck while the skill stays put. That is a defect in the
> criterion, not in the skill — recorded in §5 rather than fixed here, because amending a bar after
> seeing results is a change the next person should make before measuring, not one I should make
> after.
>
> **The condition is right. `n=3` could not measure it, and this is the plan's most useful finding.**
> Read at n=3 the count went three → one → two without any skill changing, because condition 4 is a
> threshold on a count of runs and three runs cannot resolve a single-digit rate. What the four
> `n=10` draws bought, per skill and with the arithmetic each sample earns:
>
> | Skill | Revisions | Rate | 95% upper bound | Condition 4 |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | `scope-architect` | 1 / 10 | 10% | 39.4% | **met** |
> | `solution-architect` | 1 / 3 | 33% | 86.5% | **met** *(and reproduced 5×)* |
> | `ba-pitch-analyzer` | 1 / 16 *(pooled, fixture never touched)* | 6.3% | 26.4% | not met |
> | `spec-evaluator` | 0 / 13 *(pooled)* | — | 20.6% | not met |
> | `task-executor` | 0 / 13 *(pooled)* | — | 20.6% | not met |
>
> **A zero is a bound, not a proof.** `0/13` says the rate is below roughly 21%; it does not say the
> skill never revises, and the report now prints that bound in the `revised?` cell rather than a
> bare `no` — which is the wording that let a 30%-miss-rate read as a finding.

## 2. Phase 0 — instrument (COMPLETE)

Built and mutation-tested. Recorded here only because the remaining phases depend on it:

- **`detector.kind: "oracle"`** — run a script, read its verdict; reuses the `path`/`equals`/
  `matches` resolution of the `json` head.
- **`detector.rows`** — score the *fraction* of an oracle's rows that pass. Without it a five-row
  contract scores 0 or 1 and the delta has two reachable values.
- **`grades: "workspace"`** — the draft is a directory; the loop builds it, the skill writes into it,
  the oracle runs against it.
- **Carry-forward** (`roundWorkspace`) — the revision round starts from the previous round's
  deliverable in a fresh directory. Exactly one file crosses.
- **`--report`** — derives `evals/DAY1-REPORT.md` from the baseline; `evals/runs/` is gitignored, so
  this is the surviving evidence.

**Phase 0b — what P2–P5 needed on top, built before any of them was measured.** Recorded here for
the same reason as the rest: these are load-bearing and none of them is a scoring choice.

- **Directory seeds and multi-path deliverables.** The seeder copied every seed to its `basename`
  and the deliverable was one file — fine for `task-executor`'s flat `todo.js`, useless for the
  other four. The Tier-1 oracles resolve their inputs through `lib/paths.mjs`
  (`shapeup/<slug>/spec/usecases/`, `.shapeup/<slug>/tasks/`), so a flattened seed lands where the
  oracle does not look and **every artifact scores 0 while the instrument reports itself healthy**.
  `deliverable` now takes a list, because `ba-pitch-analyzer` writes a spec tree AND a board and
  those live under two different storage roots (ADR-0001); naming only the first would carry half
  of every revision forward. The carry-forward hash is a sorted (path, sha) digest over the whole
  tree, so the no-improvement ratchet still watches the thing that determines the score.
- **Two row renderers** — `evals/oracles/lint-rows.mjs` (wraps `spec-lint` / `trace-lint` /
  `board-derive`) and `evals/oracles/verdict-rows.mjs` (wraps the `process` oracle). The Tier-1
  oracles print JSON reports; `detector.rows` needs `PASS <id> <label>` lines. Both import the real
  scripts rather than re-implementing a rule, because a second implementation of a rule is a second
  rule and the two drift.
- **Vacuous truth is not a pass**, and it is the one hazard a lint-delegated rubric has that an
  oracle-that-runs-the-code does not. `spec-lint` reports no PA1 finding for a `scopes/` directory
  that is empty; `trace-lint` reports no unreachable engine for a wiring map with no rows. Read
  straight, an artifact that was never written scores 3 of 3 on the delegated rules — and the score
  **rises with every rule delegated**. Every profile therefore fails all of its rows when its
  subject is absent, and structural §48(d1b) asserts a seed-only workspace scores exactly `0`.

> **This was not a hypothetical.** The first version of the wiring renderer excluded engines whose
> path did not resolve from the reachability row, to keep it distinct from the row that reports
> *why*. On a paid P3 run that scored a map **5 of 6 green while six of six engines had never been
> resolved at all** — reachability reported green having verified nothing. Fixed, declared, and
> P3 re-measured under the corrected instrument with both numbers published (§4 P3).

> **Still deliberately NOT built:** a model-judge head for Tier 1. It would re-introduce the
> judgement this tier exists to remove. The judge head stays a Tier-2 tool, for skills with no oracle.

## 3. The shared fixture spine

One seed, exercised by four skills **in pipeline order** — the order a real run uses.

```
idea.md ──▶ ba-pitch-analyzer ──▶ spec tree + board
                                      ├──▶ solution-architect ──▶ wiring-map.md
                                      └──▶ scope-architect ────▶ scopes/*.md
                                                    └──▶ task-executor ──▶ code
                                                                └──▶ spec-evaluator ──▶ verdict
```

Each stage's output is the next stage's input, so one authored artifact grounds all five
measurements and keeps authored ground truth to one file instead of five.

**Risk:** a shared spine correlates the measurements — a poor board makes `scope-architect` score
badly through no fault of its own. **Mitigation:** every stage after the first runs against a
**frozen committed reference input**, not the previous stage's live output. The pipeline is the
*fixture's* shape, not the measurement's dependency graph.

**Second risk, learned in Phase 1:** the seed must state every interface the oracles probe. Before
any stage is measured, `idea.md` and each stage's prompt are audited against §0's rule.

#### P1 result — the confound was the whole delta, and removing it exposed a ceiling

Figures are in [`evals/DAY1-REPORT.md`](../../evals/DAY1-REPORT.md); this is the reading of them.

Phase 1 argued that every point of the old `+0.267` came from the model inferring two conventions
the spec never stated. That is a falsifiable prediction: state them, and the delta should collapse
to zero. **It did, exactly.** All three first drafts now honour the interface and pass all six rows
on the first attempt — v1 1.0 → final 1.0, delta 0, approve 3/3. The old "improvement" was
convention discovery, and the fixture had been measuring the apparatus.

So P1's exit criterion is **half met, and the honest half is the one that failed**. Both numbers are
published side by side and every graded row now traces to the spec — but the delta does not measure
edge-case handling either, because there is no longer anything for it to measure. **`task-executor`
is now ceilinged exactly as `spec-evaluator` is**: the revision step never ran, so Day 1 is met on
its letter (`final_score` clears the bar) and not on Table II's spirit (a *measured improvement*).
The headroom that made the old number look like a working loop was the confound itself.

That is why **P5′** exists above. Two of two measured skills now approve on the first draft, which
makes "the fixtures are too easy" the single most important open item on this rung — and it is a
fixture problem, not a rubric problem, in both cases.

#### P5′ result — a harder fixture did *not* break the ceiling, and that is the finding

The replacement fixture (`fixtures/idea-hard.md` + `todo-hard.contract.json`, 12 rows) adds batch
selectors: `rm 1 3 5`, `done 2-4`, `rm 2-3 5`, resolved against the list *as it was when the command
started*, with one bad selector refusing the whole batch. The classic failure is splicing in
ascending index order so each removal shifts the items not yet removed — the defect the committed
`partial-hard.js` fixture carries, which scores 0.75 against the same oracle.

The fixture demonstrably has headroom: **weak 0.0 < partial 0.75 < strong 1.0**, and a stub-driven
loop revises 0.75 → 1.0 in two rounds. It did not matter. `claude-sonnet-5` scored **1.0 on the
first draft in all three runs** and every draft passes all 12 rows on re-scoring, so the ceiling is
genuine and not a scoring artifact.

**The conclusion is about the axis, not the difficulty dial.** Headroom is a property of *task and
model jointly*. Making a fully-specified CLI task harder does not create headroom against a model
that can do it; it only raises the bar the model clears first try. Two rounds of fixture escalation
(P1's six rows, P5′'s twelve) produced the same ceiling, which is enough evidence to stop pulling
that lever for this skill on this model.

What follows is that **condition 4 must be sought where headroom exists** — a weaker model, or a
task class this skill genuinely struggles with. That is not a lowered bar: a quality rate is
model-dependent, this repo states so beside every number, and demonstrating that the loop measures a
*real* improvement on one skill+model pair is exactly what Day 1's rung asks for and what nothing in
this repo has ever shown.

Two findings from the run worth carrying into P2–P5:

- **The first attempt aborted with 7 permission denials**, because the adapter granted
  `Bash(node:*)` — a prefix pattern matches the whole command string, so it denies the compound
  script a session writes to test its own work. Nothing was scored. Pre-P0 that output would have
  been graded as skill quality; it is FC-02, the same cause as the withdrawn −0.571 measurement.
  The grant is now `Bash`, which matches production, where task-executor has a shell for its T0
  probes. **Any new fixture must confirm its tool grant before its first paid run.**
- **Stale drafts are now cleared per skill+model before a run.** The aborted attempt left two fresh
  drafts beside twelve old ones, and the stale files were analysed as if they were the new
  measurement — a wrong conclusion drawn from the instrument's own leftovers, where the files
  differed only by mtime.

**v1 0.333 → final 1.0, delta +0.667, improved 2/3, approve 3/3, and two of three runs needed a
second round.** Every one of §1's four conditions holds. Nothing in this repo had ever shown the
fourth: `spec-evaluator` and `task-executor` between them had produced twelve paid runs across two
models and two fixture escalations without the revision step executing once.

What a round actually did, from the stored artifacts:

| Run | Round 1 | The defect the oracle named | Round 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0 | the entries table was not under a `## Wiring` heading, so the map parsed as zero rows | 1.0 |
| 2 | 1.0 | — | *(approved)* |
| 3 | 0.0 | same | 1.0 |

**Both numbers, side by side, and why there are two.** The first measurement of this fixture read
v1 0.611 → final 0.667, delta +0.056, approve 2/3. It is retained in the report under
`superseded` (fixture `48943f1ee789b6e9` → `8bfbe80b203aeecd`) and the two must not be subtracted:
the instrument changed between them. Specifically, `WM-REACH` used to exclude engines whose path
did not resolve, so it reported *"all 6 engines reach the entry point"* for a map in which six of
six had never been resolved — a green row whose own evidence described nothing. The fix makes the
row **stricter**, and the score went **up**, because the lenient version had been handing a
half-passing round-1 draft 0.833 and letting one run stop early.

**What was NOT changed, and this is the load-bearing part.** Two failure modes looked like
apparatus artifacts and the temptation was to rewrite the prompt around them: the `## Wiring`
heading, and an `engine` cell written as `` src/capture/add.js (`addTodo`) `` instead of a bare
path. Rewriting a fixture after seeing which rows failed is the retuning §7.1 forbids, and §0's
derivability audit rules against it on both counts — the heading is stated in the fixture prompt,
and the bare path is stated in the skill's own `SKILL.md` (*"repo-relative path — name the real
path"*). **The fixture was not touched.** The rule is only worth having if it also rules against
changes you want to make.

**The honest reading of what improved.** These are conformance defects — a heading, a cell format —
not reasoning failures. The loop demonstrably moved a real score by acting on a criterion-level
defect, which is what Table II asks for and what nothing here had shown; it did not demonstrate
that the skill *reasoned better* on the second pass. Both things are true and the second one is
what the next rung is for.

#### What was built — and how 9 authored criteria became 3

Both halves shipped. `examples/eval-planted-bug-2/` carries the fixture, `README.md` beside it
explains what is planted and why there are two of them.

**The rubric reduction is the more interesting half, because it looked impossible.** A verdict is
not lint-able: it is right or wrong only relative to what the build actually does, which is why the
pilot ended up with nine hand-written criteria in the first place. But there *is* an oracle that can
answer here — it just answers a different question. The `process` oracle knows the **ground truth**
about the build. So `evals/oracles/verdict-rows.mjs` runs it, reads the judge's envelope, and grades
**agreement**: one row per Test-Surface row, passing when the judge's verdict for that row matches
what the build does. Nobody authored those rows; `todo.contract.json` did.

The property that buys is not just fewer criteria. **A false-FAIL now costs exactly what a
false-PASS costs** — a judge that condemns a row the build passes is wrong in the same currency as
one that clears a row the build fails. Nine `must`/`must_not` criteria written around one known
planted bug can only ever detect leniency, which is half an instrument.

Three rows stay authored, each citing the line of `SKILL.md` it comes from: `JV-REFUTED`,
`JV-ESCALATED`, `JV-NO-CLOSURE`.

**The fixture's three arms**, all stated in `spec/usecases/UC-01.md`'s Test Surface:

| | What it catches |
|---|---|
| **TS-04** | `done 99` exits 0 in silence. Reachable with **no store file** — the obvious probe finds it. |
| **TS-06** | `rm 2-3` splices in ascending order and deletes `b` and `d`. Reachable **only** against a seeded four-item store. Every other row passes on a fresh install, and the build's own suite is green precisely because every case in it runs on one. |
| **the open question** | `archive` with zero done items — two incompatible readings, no Test-Surface row. The contract is to RAISE it, not settle it. Choosing an answer and grading against the choice is the confident-wrong move, and it reads as diligence. |

`todo.contract.json` is deliberately **not** seeded into the workspace. It is the probes, and
handing them over — the seeded store included — would remove the headroom the fixture exists to

  Reading the obvious one would have published spend ~50× under the truth while looking
  well-sourced. Structural §48(d8) pins this with a canned envelope carrying both.
- **A failed round still spends.** The max-turns probe that established the error envelope's shape
  cost $0.0128 and produced no draft, so aborts now report what the failed round spent, and the
  register of what a re-run costs includes the runs that produced nothing.
- Unknown spend is withheld, never summed: if any round or run fails to report, the total is `null`
  with a `partial` source and the per-round figures survive beside it. A partial sum renders exactly
  like a measured total, which is the F1 sin applied to spend.
- Free side-catch: the envelope reports `permission_denials` as data, so **FC-02 is now mechanical**.
  The withdrawn −0.571 measurement in the report was a session denied `node`, correctly refusing to
  fabricate an observation, scored as skill quality. That now aborts instead of being graded.

Eight mutations were applied and each was confirmed to fail its control (P0's share of the
structural suite's 950 → 979). Two are worth naming because they are the ones that would have
shipped silently: dropping
`--output-format json`, after which every other check still passes while the spend column quietly
empties; and recording cost only for rounds the loop KEPT, which under-bills every run that stopped
on the no-improvement ratchet. **The one figure P0 does not produce is a retroactive cost for the
runs already taken** — those stay `not reported` in the report's spend table.

Then P1: it closes the only open item on a skill already built, and its lesson (§0) is a
precondition for authoring P2–P5's fixtures well. **P1 is the first phase whose ~$21 estimate can be
checked against what it actually cost**, and that comparison should be published with its result.

## 6. What this plan will NOT cover, and why

**Tier 3 — six skills that document their own lack of ground truth.** `qa-edge-hunter` *"never issues
a verdict"*; `scope-hammer` compares *"never against a perfect ideal"*; `coach` files rules *"after
asking the PO to categorize each one — never assumed"*. Plus `advisor-protocol`, `shapeup`, `orient`
— adjudication and exploration.

Forcing a Day-1 number onto these means inventing ground truth their authors deliberately declined to
define. §VIII.A.1: *"Can success be verified? If not, do not begin with autonomy."*

**`tech-lead` — right rung, wrong measurement.** Its output is a *run*, not an artifact; you cannot
revise a run, only start another. It belongs at Table III's **workflow layer** (task success, cost),
which `sdd-harness-bench` already measures.

**The honest ceiling is 6 of 13** (5 Tier-1 + `translator`), not 13/13. **5 of 13 are now
instrumented and measured** — the whole of Tier 1. `translator` is the one remaining skill inside
that ceiling and this plan does not cover it (Tier 2, §Scope).

## 7. Risks

1. **The oracle becomes the target** (§IX.B). Optimising a skill to satisfy `spec-lint` is not the
   same as good slicing. **Mitigation — the no-retuning rule:** the oracles were written before this
   measurement existed and are **not to be relaxed to raise a score.** Where a fixture must change
   (P1), the change is declared before the run and both numbers are published.
2. **The apparatus becomes the target.** Phase 1's actual failure, and the more insidious one,
   because it survives full oracle delegation. Mitigated by §0's spec-derivability audit, run before
   each measurement rather than after.
   > **It came up again in P3, and the audit is what settled it.** Two rows failed on things that
   > look like apparatus at first glance: the `## Wiring` heading `contract-md.mjs` parses the
   > entries table under, and an `engine` cell written as `` src/capture/add.js (`addTodo`) ``
   > rather than a bare path. The temptation is to call both fixture defects and rewrite the prompt
   > — which, after seeing which rows failed, is exactly the retuning §7.1 forbids. The audit says
   > otherwise on both: the heading is stated in the fixture prompt, and the bare path is stated in
   > the skill's OWN contract (`SKILL.md` → *"repo-relative path — name the real path"*). Neither
   > row can only be passed by inferring how the harness probes. **The fixture was not changed.**
   > The rule earns its keep by ruling *against* a change as often as for one.

2b. **The instrument's own vacuous truth.** A hazard §0 does not name and P3 hit live: a lint
   reports nothing for an artifact that is not there, so a delegated rule can be green having
   checked nothing. Every renderer now fails all its rows when its subject is absent, structural
   §48(d1b) pins it, and the one that slipped through was caught only because a paid run produced
   a 5-of-6 score whose evidence line said six of six engines had never resolved. **A green row
   whose evidence describes nothing is the shape to look for.**
3. **Correlated fixtures.** One spine grounds five measurements; a flaw in `idea.md` biases all of
   them. Mitigated by frozen reference inputs per stage (§3), not eliminated.
4. **Structural conformance read as quality.** Two of these five (`ba`, partly `solution-architect`)
   can only measure well-formedness. The caveat rides in `per_skill_notes`, which §48 forces into the
   report — not only in this document, which nobody re-reads.
5. **Cost overrun on code generation.** `task-executor` at `--max-turns 60` across 3 runs × up to 5
   rounds is the most expensive item. Cap it and report the cap: the cap defines what was measured.
   > **Did not materialise, and the reason inverts the risk.** `task-executor` was the CHEAPEST of
   > the five at **$0.93**, against $2.18–$2.68 for the four that write markdown. The cap was never
   > the binding constraint because no fixture needed five rounds — the longest run here was two.
   > What actually drives spend is the **per-round cache-read of the session preamble**: P2–P5 each
   > moved 2.2–3.5M cache-read tokens against 25–67k output tokens, so a round costs roughly what it
   > costs regardless of how much work it contains. The correct lever for a Day-1 budget is
   > therefore *rounds × runs*, not turn cap or output length. Every measured figure is in the
   > report's spend table.
